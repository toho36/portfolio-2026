## A. Lunar Genesis, the original big gesture

Visual subject: a clearly recognisable proto-Earth and an emerging Moon. Use the NASA/Durham Moon-formation sequence as choreography reference, not a borrowed finished portfolio asset or a runtime physics solver. It depicts a Moon-origin hypothesis, not the creation of Earth.

First frame: large, cropped, shadowed planetary body. We enter just AFTER contact, not from two tiny balls flying at each other. One broad incandescent material tongue already links the main body and smaller remnant. No fireworks or particle fog.

Opening proposal, approximately 4 seconds: the material tongue stretches, necks and separates; the smaller remnant settles into an unmistakable satellite relationship. Orange illumination recedes. This is a deliberately compressed artistic sequence, not a claim of scientific timescale or a physically proved simulation. No sudden lava-to-blue-ocean transformation.

Settled hero: graphite/muted-mineral planet with visible land-like relief rather than contemporary bright-blue Earth, warm terminator and a distinctly smaller rocky satellite. Subject fills roughly two thirds of the screen; identity and short positioning occupy one small quiet reading zone. Desaturated palette bridges to ivory/orange/dark-green project cards. Normal present-day Earth is a different final-art option, not silently equivalent to the proto-Earth motif.

Scroll handoff: the whole space composition goes above the viewport as the existing deck enters from below. The world does not morph into a watch or pretend to have birthed software projects.

Why it could stand out: actual causal transformation, recognisable phenomenon and clear arrival state. Why it could fail: four seconds may still be too much for an intelligible separation; mediocre fluid/material work would look like a game cinematic, and the astronomy theme has no established personal connection. Big quality and thematic risk. Pick it only if the owner wants a cosmic signature, not simply because the render is possible.

## B. Earth / Moon, a cinematic orbital portrait

Visual subject: contemporary Earth and Moon, treated like an extremely disciplined photographic composition, not a sci-fi dashboard.

First frame: close curved Earth horizon with a thin, physically lit atmospheric edge and recognisable cloud/continental structure. Never an empty black screen. Earth initially extends beyond the bottom/right frame. Name is already present at modest scale, not the cinematic reward.

Brainstorm správně pojmenoval selhání, ale zatím nabízí spíš scénáře pohybu než přesvědčivé obrazové kompozice. **B nemá automaticky náskok tím, že je méně riskantní než A.** Může být snazší vyrobit a přitom méně osobité.

Materiály a osvětlení navíc připomínají pracovní 3D náhled.
- **Signature:** Jméno je velké, ale nemá vlastní typografický charakter. Těsné rozestupy, téměř slepené „VIET TO“ a tvrdý černý stín vytvářejí těžký blok. Drobná identita v navigaci, popisek nad jménem a slogan pod ním opakují informace bez jasné potřeby. Prázdná pravá část kompozici nevyvažuje. Samotné zvětšení serifu zde nahrazuje výtvarné rozhodnutí.

Moje předchozí doporučení anonymního objektu a velkého serifu tyto výsledky neobhajuje. Oba směry v této podobě selhaly.
